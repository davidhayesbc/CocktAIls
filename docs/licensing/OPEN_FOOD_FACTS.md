# Open Food Facts product lookup

CocktAIls can optionally query the Open Food Facts v3.6 single-product read endpoint after the user taps **Look up on Open Food Facts**. Scanning or typing a barcode alone does not make a network request. The request contains the barcode in the URL and a CocktAIls/version User-Agent; Open Food Facts also receives the ordinary network IP address. The request does not include pantry contents, household/member data, locations, photos, credentials, or other form fields.

The lookup asks only for `code`, `product_name`, `brands`, `quantity`, `product_quantity`, `product_quantity_unit`, `last_modified_t`, and `schema_version`. It does not ask for images, nutrition, or categories. Brand, product name, and quantity are shown as provider suggestions for review. They are not copied into the household ingredient/product fields; ingredient name, category, brand, and product-name fields remain separately entered household data. Category remains manually selected, and ABV is not retrieved or stored. Quantity is source text and is not treated as a verified bottle volume.

Open Food Facts distinguishes its database license (ODbL 1.0) from its contents license (DbCL). The app attributes returned data as **Open Food Facts · ODbL 1.0 / DbCL** beside the suggestion and does not include images. This documentation is not a blanket legal clearance statement; see the provider's [API introduction](https://openfoodfacts.github.io/documentation/docs/Product-Opener/api/), [license guidance](https://openfoodfacts.github.io/documentation/docs/Product-Opener/api/tutorials/license-be-on-the-legal-side/), and [ODbL 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/).

Successful results are cached in a dedicated app-private preferences file for 30 days, with a maximum of 40 records. This cache is separate from the household database, revision history, and household backup/export format. Restoring a household backup on another device does not restore Open Food Facts cache entries; another explicit lookup may be needed. The cache records the requested and returned barcode codes, fetched time, upstream modification timestamp, schema version, and warning IDs so a displayed cached suggestion keeps its source context. Failed, missing, malformed, invalid-code, and rate-limited responses are not cached. Images are neither requested nor cached.

The client uses HTTPS, five-second connect/read timeouts, a 256 KiB response limit, no automatic retries or redirects, and a local limit of five uncached requests per minute with a three-second minimum gap. HTTP 429 is surfaced for manual retry later. Unknown response fields are ignored; malformed envelopes and failure-impact errors fail safely to manual entry. Provider coverage, including spirits, is incomplete and is not represented as comprehensive.
